Mutual Fund Portfolio Rebalancing Strategies

Discover how maintaining and rebalancing mutual fund portfolios can secure long-term wealth.

FA

By FinAI Editorial

Updated August 08, 2026

Portfolio rebalancing is the process of realigning the weightings of a portfolio of assets. It involves periodically buying or selling assets in a portfolio to maintain an original or desired level of asset allocation or risk.

For investors actively managing mutual fund portfolios, redemptions and purchases are essential tools. As markets fluctuate, the original target allocation (e.g., 60% Equity, 40% Debt) can drift. Rebalancing forces an investor to sell high and buy low.

Key Takeaways

  • Rebalancing helps maintain your original risk tolerance.
  • It requires ongoing management, including calculated purchases and redemptions.
  • Most experts recommend reviewing mutual fund portfolios at least once a year.

What Is Portfolio Rebalancing?

Imagine you start with ₹1,00,000 allocated 70% to equity mutual funds and 30% to debt. A bull market pushes your equity portion to 85% of your total value. Your portfolio is now significantly riskier than you initially planned. Rebalancing involves redeeming some equity units and purchasing debt units to return to the 70/30 split.

How to Execute Purchases and Redemptions

There are two primary ways to rebalance:

  1. Cash Flow Rebalancing: Directing new investments (purchases) into the underweighted asset class (e.g., buying more debt funds).
  2. Sell and Buy: Redeeming units from the overweighted class and using the proceeds to buy the underweighted class.
